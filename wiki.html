1. Standard Missile

TACTICAL ORDNANCE

The Standard Missile is a tactical missile built for medium-range engagements. It mounts onto the 3x3 Launch Pad and uses physics-driven projectile trajectory mechanics directed by the Coordinate Terminal.

  • • LAUNCH MODE: Fires directly from 65° inclined rails (no vertical elevation required)
  • • FLIGHT SPEED: 0.35 → 1.5 blocks/tick climb; 2.0 blocks/tick terminal attack dive
  • • BLAST STATS: 8.0 Explosion Power | Up to 160 DMG (6m), lethal shockwave out to 35m | 90m Seismic Shake
  • • AUDIO RANGE: 200-block dynamic flyby sound & incoming target warning

2. Heavy Missile

HIGH-YIELD STRATEGIC

The Heavy Missile is a high-yield strategic weapon. Upon launch, it climbs to high altitude before entering a steep terminal descent onto its target coordinates.

Impact produces a massive crater, shockwave, shattered glass, and scorched ground. Due to its extreme descent speed, automated Anti-Air Systems struggle to intercept it in time.

  • • LAUNCH REQUIREMENT: Must be raised to 90° vertical position (Right-click pad with empty hand)
  • • ARMOR RATING: Reinforced hull (2 Armor Points) — survives a single Interceptor hit!
  • • YIELD & EFFECTS: Unblockable sphere crater | Thermal burn & scorched blocks | 140m Earthquake & Blinding Flash
  • • AUDIO RANGE: 300-block flyby roar & dual-layer long-range detonation audio

// TACTICAL DOCTRINE & TIPS

Anti-Air System vs. Heavy Missile Dynamics

Understanding the interaction between Anti-Air Systems and the two offensive missile classes is critical for both base defense and strategic strikes:

Against Standard Missiles

The Anti-Air System is very effective against Standard Missiles. Because Standard Missiles operate on a medium-range tactical profile, automated Interceptor Missiles can reliably track and shoot them down in a single hit before impact.

Against Heavy Missiles

The Anti-Air System struggles against the extreme descent speed and reinforced hull (2 Armor Points) of Heavy Missiles. Because a Heavy Missile climbs to high altitude and plunges down in a steep terminal descent, a single AA battery rarely has time to land two intercept hits before detonation.
